Essential trace mineral

Zinc, the repair mineral

Zinc is an essential mineral that hundreds of enzymes need, and it supports immune function, wound healing, making DNA and protein, and taste1.

MineralsTracked in mgDaily target3 sources · checked 2026-09-27

How much you need

01 What it does

What zinc does for you.

Immune defence

Zinc helps your immune system fight off invading bacteria and viruses2.

Wound healing

Zinc plays a role in wound healing, and low zinc in older adults can slow it1.

DNA and protein

Your body uses zinc to make DNA and proteins and for cell division1.

Growth

Zinc supports growth during pregnancy, infancy, childhood and adolescence1.

Taste and smell

Zinc is involved in the sense of taste, and low zinc can dull taste and smell1.

02 How your body uses it

How your body uses zinc.

Zinc balance is kept by absorbing zinc from food, releasing it into the gut and reabsorbing it; as intake rises, the share absorbed falls1.

Phytates in beans and whole grains lower zinc absorption, and soaking beans, grains and seeds before cooking may help1. Amounts per serving from1.

Fortified cereals

Breakfast cereals are often fortified with zinc and are a major source of zinc in the U.S. diet1.

Lozenges and creams

Some cold lozenges, nasal sprays and denture creams contain zinc; heavy use of such creams can cause toxicity1.

05 Balance

Too little, too much.

Signs like these have many possible causes. They are a reason to talk to a clinician, not to self-treat.

Too little

Zinc deficiency affects many tissues, and its signs vary with age1.

  • Frequent infections
  • Loss of taste and smell
  • Slow wound healing
  • Hair loss
  • Poor appetite
  • Slow growth in children

Too much

High zinc intakes, usually from supplements rather than food, can cause side effects1.

  • Nausea and vomiting
  • Stomach upset or cramps
  • Headaches and dizziness
  • Low copper status over time
  • Weaker immune function
  • Lower HDL cholesterol
06 Blood test

Serum zinc.

Serum or plasma zinc is often used to assess zinc status, but it shifts with time of day, infection and other factors, so it is read alongside risk factors1.

80–120 µg/dLTypical in healthy people
Below 70 µg/dL (women)Suggests inadequate zinc status

Labs use different units and ranges. Your clinician reads a result alongside your history and other tests.

07 Supplements

Supplement forms.

Supplement labels list elemental zinc, and common forms include zinc sulfate, acetate and gluconate1.

Zinc gluconate

Absorbed at about 61% in young adults, similar to zinc citrate1.

Zinc citrate

Absorbed about as well as zinc gluconate1.

Zinc oxide

About 50% absorbed in the same comparison1.

Zinc sulfate or acetate

Also widely used; it is not clear that any one form is better than the others2.

Supplements can interact with medicines. Talk to a clinician or pharmacist before you start one, especially at high doses.

08 Key points

Remember these five.

  1. Oysters, meat and seafood provide zinc that your body absorbs well.
  2. Zinc from beans and grains is absorbed less because of phytates.
  3. Soaking beans, grains and seeds before cooking may improve zinc absorption.
  4. High-dose zinc taken for weeks can lower copper status.
  5. Large iron supplements taken at the same time can reduce zinc absorption.
FAQ

Questions about zinc.

Does zinc help with colds?

Study results are mixed. Zinc lozenges or syrup started early in a cold may shorten how long symptoms last, but they do not seem to make symptoms less severe.

Can vegetarians get enough zinc?

Yes, with some planning. Zinc from beans and grains is less available because of phytates, so soaking, fermenting or choosing canned beans can help, and some people may benefit from a supplement.

Why can too much zinc cause low copper?

Taken for weeks, high doses of zinc interfere with copper absorption, which can lead to low copper status. This usually comes from supplements or heavy use of zinc-containing denture creams, not from food.
