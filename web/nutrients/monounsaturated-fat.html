Heart-healthy fat

Monounsaturated fat, the olive oil fat

Monounsaturated fat, found in olive oil, avocados and nuts, can help lower LDL (bad) cholesterol when it replaces saturated or trans fat1.

01 What it does

What monounsaturated fat does for you.

Helps lower LDL

Monounsaturated fats can help lower your LDL (bad) cholesterol level1.

Builds your cells

They help develop and maintain your cells1.

A smart swap

Replacing saturated fat with unsaturated fats may lower LDL and heart disease risk234.

Energy dense

Like all fats, they carry 9 calories per gram, over twice that of carbs or protein1.

02 How your body uses it

How your body uses monounsaturated fat.

Bile and lipase break fat into fatty acids that travel in chylomicrons through lymph into the blood56. Muscle and fat tissue then burn them for energy or store them8.

05 Balance

Too little, too much.

Signs like these have many possible causes. They are a reason to talk to a clinician, not to self-treat.

Too much

All fats contain 9 calories per gram, so eating too much fat, even healthy fat, can lead to weight gain1.

  • Extra calories add up quickly
  • Weight gain over time
  • Nuts are calorie-dense
06 Key points

Remember these five.

  1. Monounsaturated fats are liquid at room temperature and start to harden when chilled.
  2. Olive, canola and peanut oils, avocados and nuts are rich sources.
  3. They can help lower LDL (bad) cholesterol when they replace saturated or trans fats.
  4. Adding them to an unhealthy diet is not enough; replacing saturated fat is what counts.
  5. All fats carry 9 calories per gram, so keep portions in check.
FAQ

Questions about monounsaturated fat.

Is olive oil a monounsaturated fat?

Yes. Olive oil is rich in monounsaturated fat, as are canola oil, avocados and many nuts. USDA data show about 10 grams of monounsaturated fat in a tablespoon of olive oil.

What is the difference between monounsaturated and polyunsaturated fat?

Both are unsaturated, healthier fats that can help lower LDL cholesterol when they replace saturated fat. They are found in different foods: olive oil, avocados and nuts for monounsaturated fat, and walnuts, seeds, fish and corn or soybean oil for polyunsaturated fat.

Can I eat as much healthy fat as I want?

No. All fats have 9 calories per gram, so too much can lead to weight gain. The benefit comes from replacing saturated and trans fats, not from adding extra fat.
