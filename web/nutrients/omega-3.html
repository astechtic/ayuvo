Essential fatty acid

Omega-3s, the membrane fats

Omega-3s are polyunsaturated fats: ALA must come from food, and EPA and DHA help build the membranes around every cell, especially in the eyes and brain2.

FatsTracked in gDaily target4 sources · checked 2026-09-27

How much you need

01 What it does

What omega-3 does for you.

Cell membranes

Omega-3s are part of the membranes that surround each cell in your body2.

Eyes

DHA levels are especially high in the retina2.

Brain

DHA is also concentrated in brain cells2.

Heart & triglycerides

Fatty fish is linked with heart health, and more EPA and DHA lowers triglyceride levels2.

Body signals

Used to make eicosanoids, signals for the heart, lungs, immune and hormone systems1.

02 How your body uses it

How your body uses omega-3s.

Omega-3 fats are absorbed about as well as other fats and travel in chylomicrons through the lymph into the blood1. The liver converts only a small share of ALA into EPA and DHA, so getting them directly from food or supplements is the practical way to raise them1.

Fortified foods

Some brands of eggs, yogurt, juices, milk, soy drinks and infant formulas have added omega-3s2.

05 Balance

Too little, too much.

Signs like these have many possible causes. They are a reason to talk to a clinician, not to self-treat.

Too little

Omega-3 deficiency is very rare in the United States, but a lack of essential fatty acids can affect the skin2.

  • Rough, scaly skin
  • Red, swollen, itchy rash
  • Dermatitis

Too much

Side effects of omega-3 supplements are usually mild, though very high doses may lengthen bleeding time1.

  • Unpleasant taste or bad breath
  • Heartburn or nausea
  • Diarrhea
  • Headache
  • Smelly sweat
  • Possible interaction with warfarin
06 Supplements

Supplement forms.

Omega-3 supplements come in several forms with widely varying doses, so check the label for the type and amount of EPA and DHA1.

Fish oil

Provides EPA and DHA; methyl mercury is removed during processing and has not been found in supplements1.

Krill oil

Provides omega-3s mainly as phospholipids; studies differ on whether they are absorbed better1.

Cod liver oil

Provides EPA and DHA plus vitamins A and D, in amounts that vary by product3.

Algal oil

A vegetarian source that usually provides DHA, and sometimes EPA1.

Flaxseed oil

Provides ALA rather than EPA or DHA3.

Supplements can interact with medicines. Talk to a clinician or pharmacist before you start one, especially at high doses.

07 Key points

Remember these five.

  1. ALA comes from plant foods; EPA and DHA come mainly from fish and seafood.
  2. Your body turns only a small amount of ALA into EPA and DHA.
  3. Flaxseed, chia seeds and walnuts are rich vegetarian sources of ALA.
  4. Algal oil is a vegetarian source of DHA.
  5. Omega-3 supplement side effects are usually mild, such as bad breath or heartburn.
FAQ

Questions about omega-3.

Can vegetarians get enough omega-3?

Most people get enough ALA from foods like flaxseed, chia seeds, walnuts and canola oil. The body converts only a little ALA into EPA and DHA, so algal oil is a vegetarian option for DHA.

Is there a blood test for omega-3?

Most clinicians do not check omega-3 status, and experts have not set normal ranges. Researchers sometimes measure EPA and DHA in red blood cells, called the omega-3 index, which reflects roughly the past four months.

Is fish oil the same as omega-3?

Fish oil is one source of the omega-3s EPA and DHA. Other supplements include krill oil, cod liver oil, algal oil and flaxseed oil, which provides ALA instead.
