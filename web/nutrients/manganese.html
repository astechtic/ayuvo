Essential trace mineral

Manganese, the quiet cofactor

Manganese is an essential trace mineral that enzymes such as manganese superoxide dismutase need for metabolism, bone formation and protecting cells from damage1.

MineralsTracked in mgDaily target2 sources · checked 2026-09-27

How much you need

01 What it does

What manganese does for you.

Metabolism

Enzymes that use manganese help process amino acids, cholesterol, glucose and carbohydrates1.

Antioxidant enzyme

Manganese superoxide dismutase helps clear reactive oxygen species that can damage cells1.

Bone formation

Manganese is a cofactor for several enzymes involved in bone formation1.

Blood clotting

Works with vitamin K in blood clotting and hemostasis1.

Immunity & reproduction

Also involved in reproduction and the immune response1.

02 How your body uses it

How your body uses manganese.

Adults absorb only about 1–5% of dietary manganese, more when intake is low; once absorbed, most is bound to transferrin, albumin and alpha-2-macroglobulin and taken up by the liver and other tissues1. More than 90% of absorbed manganese leaves in bile, which keeps tissue levels steady1.

Meat, poultry, eggs and dairy provide little or no manganese, and grain products, tea and vegetables are the top sources for U.S. adults1. Amounts per serving from1.

Tea and spices

Tea is one of the top manganese sources for U.S. adults, and spices such as black pepper add some too1.

Drinking water

Drinking water contains small amounts, about 1 to 100 µg per litre1.

Supplements

Not all multivitamins contain manganese; those that do typically have 1.0 to 4.5 mg1.

05 Balance

Too little, too much.

Signs like these have many possible causes. They are a reason to talk to a clinician, not to self-treat.

Too little

Manganese deficiency is very rare and its signs are not firmly established; very limited evidence links it with these1.

  • Weak bones and poor growth in children
  • Skin rashes
  • Loss of hair colour
  • Mood changes
  • Worse premenstrual pain
  • Abnormal glucose tolerance

Too much

No harm has been shown from manganese in food, but toxicity has occurred from inhaling manganese dust at work and from drinking water with very high levels1.

  • Tremors and muscle spasms
  • Ringing in the ears, hearing loss
  • Feeling unsteady on your feet
  • Mood changes and irritability
  • Trouble sleeping
  • Parkinson-like movement problems
06 Supplements

Supplement forms.

Supplement labels list elemental manganese, and no data are available on how well the different forms are absorbed1.

Amino acid chelates

Such as manganese bisglycinate, manganese glycinate and manganese aspartate1.

Manganese sulfate

A common form; no form is known to be better than any other2.

Gluconate, citrate, picolinate

Other forms found in supplements, along with manganese chloride1.

Supplements can interact with medicines. Talk to a clinician or pharmacist before you start one, especially at high doses.

07 Key points

Remember these five.

  1. Whole grains, nuts, legumes, leafy greens, shellfish and tea supply most dietary manganese.
  2. Meat, poultry, eggs and dairy provide little or none.
  3. The body absorbs only a small share of the manganese in food and removes extra through bile.
  4. Low iron status can increase how much manganese is absorbed.
  5. Manganese toxicity has come from workplace dust and contaminated water, not from food.
FAQ

Questions about manganese.

Is manganese the same as magnesium?

No. They are different minerals with similar names. Manganese is a trace mineral that enzymes need in small amounts, while magnesium is needed in much larger amounts and has its own guide.

Who is more sensitive to excess manganese?

People with chronic liver disease clear manganese through bile less well, and iron deficiency increases manganese absorption, so both can make excess manganese more likely to cause problems. Breathing manganese dust at work, as in welding and mining, is another known risk.

Does tea provide manganese?

Yes. A cup of brewed black tea provides about 0.5 mg of manganese, and tea is one of the top sources of manganese for U.S. adults, along with grain products and vegetables.
