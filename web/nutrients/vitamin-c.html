Water-soluble vitamin

Vitamin C, the collagen builder

Vitamin C is a water-soluble antioxidant the body cannot make, needed for collagen, immune function and absorbing iron from plant foods1.

VitaminsTracked in mgDaily target3 sources · checked 2026-09-27

How much you need

01 What it does

What vitamin C does for you.

Builds collagen

Needed to make collagen, a protein that supports connective tissue and helps wounds heal2.

Antioxidant

Helps protect cells from free-radical damage and regenerates vitamin E1.

Plant iron uptake

Improves absorption of non-heme iron, the form found in plant foods1.

Immune function

Plays an important role in immune function1.

Carnitine & messengers

Required to make L-carnitine and certain neurotransmitters1.

02 How your body uses it

How your body uses vitamin C.

A dedicated transporter absorbs vitamin C, and the body keeps plasma and tissue levels in a tight range1. At large doses absorption falls and unused vitamin C is excreted in the urine1.

Fortified foods

05 Balance

Too little, too much.

Signs like these have many possible causes. They are a reason to talk to a clinician, not to self-treat.

Too little

Deficiency is rare, but very low intake for many weeks can cause scurvy, which weakens connective tissue2.

  • Fatigue
  • Swollen, bleeding gums
  • Small red or purple skin spots
  • Joint pain
  • Poor wound healing
  • Corkscrew hairs

Too much

Vitamin C has low toxicity, but high doses can cause stomach upset from unabsorbed vitamin C in the gut1.

  • Diarrhea
  • Nausea
  • Stomach cramps
  • Possible kidney stone risk
  • Worse iron overload if prone
06 Blood test

Plasma vitamin C.

Vitamin C status is usually assessed by measuring plasma vitamin C; white-blood-cell levels may be more accurate but are harder to measure reliably1.

Labs use different units and ranges. Your clinician reads a result alongside your history and other tests.

07 Supplements

Supplement forms.

Supplements usually contain ascorbic acid, which is as bioavailable as the vitamin C naturally in foods1.

Ascorbic acid

The most common form; as well absorbed as vitamin C from orange juice or broccoli1.

Mineral ascorbates

Sodium and calcium ascorbate; research has not shown any form to be better than others2.

With bioflavonoids

One study found no difference in blood levels versus plain ascorbic acid1.

Supplements can interact with medicines. Talk to a clinician or pharmacist before you start one, especially at high doses.

08 Key points

Remember these five.

  1. Your body cannot make or store much vitamin C, so regular intake from food matters.
  2. Raw fruit and vegetables keep more vitamin C than cooked or long-stored ones.
  3. Pairing vitamin C foods with beans or greens helps you absorb plant iron.
  4. People who smoke need more vitamin C than people who do not.
  5. Very large doses are absorbed less well and can upset your stomach.
FAQ

Questions about vitamin C.

Does vitamin C prevent colds?

For most people, regular vitamin C supplements do not lower the chance of catching a cold. People who take them regularly may have slightly shorter or milder colds. Starting vitamin C after symptoms begin does not appear to help.

Does cooking destroy vitamin C?

Yes, vitamin C dissolves in water and breaks down with heat, so boiling and long storage lower the amount in food. Steaming or microwaving may reduce the losses, and many rich sources are eaten raw.

Which foods have more vitamin C than an orange?

Half a cup of raw red sweet pepper has more vitamin C than a medium orange, and orange juice is similar to red pepper. Kiwifruit, green pepper, broccoli and strawberries are also rich sources.
