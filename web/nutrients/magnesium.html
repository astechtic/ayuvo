Essential mineral

Magnesium, the enzyme helper

Magnesium is a mineral that helps more than 300 enzyme systems, supporting energy production, muscle and nerve function, blood sugar, blood pressure and bone1.

MineralsTracked in mgDaily target3 sources · checked 2026-09-27

How much you need

01 What it does

What magnesium does for you.

Energy production

Needed for energy production and a cofactor in more than 300 enzyme systems1.

Muscles & nerves

Helps regulate muscle and nerve function2.

Heart rhythm

Helps keep your heartbeat steady3.

Bone structure

Contributes to bone development; 50–60% of body magnesium sits in bone1.

Blood sugar & pressure

Helps regulate blood sugar levels and blood pressure2.

02 How your body uses it

How your body uses magnesium.

About 30–40% of the magnesium in food is absorbed, and less than 1% of body magnesium is in the blood1. The kidneys control balance, excreting extra in urine and holding on to more when status is low1.

Tap, mineral and bottled waters can add magnesium, but the amount varies widely by source and brand1.

Fortified foods

Magnesium is added to some breakfast cereals and other fortified foods1.

RDA (Recommended Dietary Allowance) is the daily amount that meets the needs of nearly all healthy people. Per day, for adults1.

Upper limit (UL) is the most per day that is unlikely to cause harm. Applies only to magnesium from supplements and medications, not magnesium in food and drinks.

The upper limit for magnesium covers only part of your intake (from supplements and medicines only, not from food), so it is not drawn on this bar.

05 Balance

Too little, too much.

Signs like these have many possible causes. They are a reason to talk to a clinician, not to self-treat.

Too little

Deficiency is uncommon in healthy people because the kidneys limit losses, but habitually low intake, some conditions, alcohol dependence or medicines can cause it1.

  • Loss of appetite
  • Nausea and vomiting
  • Fatigue and weakness
  • Numbness and tingling
  • Muscle cramps
  • Abnormal heart rhythm

Too much

Magnesium from food isn't harmful for healthy kidneys, but high doses from supplements or medicines can cause side effects1.

  • Diarrhea
  • Nausea
  • Abdominal cramping
  • Low blood pressure (very high doses)
  • Irregular heartbeat (toxicity)
06 Blood test

Serum magnesium.

Serum magnesium is the most common test, but it reflects body stores poorly because most magnesium is inside cells and bone1.

Serum magnesiumWhat it may mean
< 0.75 mmol/LLow (hypomagnesemia)
0.75–0.95 mmol/LNormal range
> 1.74–2.61 mmol/LSigns of toxicity usually appear

Labs use different units and ranges. Your clinician reads a result alongside your history and other tests.

07 Supplements

Supplement forms.

Labels list elemental magnesium, and forms that dissolve well in liquid are absorbed more completely than less soluble forms1.

Magnesium citrate

Absorbed more completely than magnesium oxide in small studies1.

Chloride, lactate, aspartate

Also more bioavailable than magnesium oxide and sulfate in small studies1.

Magnesium oxide

A common, less bioavailable form; oxide is among the forms most often linked to diarrhea1.

Laxatives & antacids

Magnesium is a key ingredient in some laxatives and heartburn remedies1.

Supplements can interact with medicines. Talk to a clinician or pharmacist before you start one, especially at high doses.

08 Key points

Remember these five.

  1. Magnesium helps more than 300 enzyme systems, including energy production.
  2. Nuts, seeds, legumes, leafy greens and whole grains are rich food sources.
  3. Refined grains lose much of their magnesium.
  4. Magnesium from food is safe for healthy kidneys; the upper limit applies to supplements.
  5. A blood test may look normal even when body stores are low.
FAQ

Questions about magnesium.

Why is the magnesium upper limit lower than the recommended amount?

The recommended amounts cover magnesium from all sources, including food and water. The upper limit applies only to magnesium from supplements and medicines, because magnesium naturally present in food isn't harmful for healthy kidneys.

Which magnesium supplement form is absorbed most completely?

Forms that dissolve well in liquid tend to be absorbed more completely. Small studies found magnesium citrate, aspartate, lactate and chloride more bioavailable than magnesium oxide and sulfate.

Can low magnesium affect calcium and potassium?

Yes. Magnesium helps move calcium and potassium across cell membranes, and severe magnesium deficiency can lead to low blood calcium or low blood potassium.
